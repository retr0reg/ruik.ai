## Interview

I interviewed Daniel Cheung at Upper Dorms, where we discussed problems and questions that he had. He doesn't have any dominating problems right now, and other than homework and a lack of personal time, life has been pretty good for him.

During this long conversation, the main idea that I absorbed is that every time after water polo practice, he has trouble walking up Gym Hill. Since the pool is next to Alamo, for him to walk up to Upper Dorm, he has to go through Gym Hill and cross Alamo. That is usually a terrible walk, since water polo takes a lot of energy from him and makes his legs very sore. He just had water polo practice before we talked, so this was the immediate problem that he had in mind.

It is also not a one-time thing. He walks that hill multiple times a day, coming back from class too, not only from practice.

## Principles

I think mental health, physical health, and fitness are very important to this person. He trains hard, and after that he just wants to rest. The hill sits right between those two things.

For the problem space, I am not really sure. I started from this one specific problem, getting a tired person up Gym Hill, and stayed there instead of defining something broader.

## Raw ideas

![Raw ideas](raw-ideas.png)
*Raw ideas*

1. Put an elevator to Gym Hill
2. Give students golf carts
3. Destroy the hill
4. Give students pressurized backpacks so they can fly
5. Ski lift
6. Allow rock climbing and put a café halfway
7. Fulton exfil system
8. Put it on the moon so it has less gravity
9. Put the campus on Gym Hill
10. Put an exoskeleton system on students

Most of these are not serious, which is the point. Three of them looked comparably practical once I went back through the list:

- **Golf carts**: the required resources already exist, so we can directly use them. What it needs is the golf carts, permission, and the ability for students to drive them.
- **Ski lift**: a real mechanism that already solves this exact problem on other hills.
- **Exoskeleton**: such technology already exists and it is not that expensive. What it needs is funding and willingness to cooperate.

## More-complete ideas

![Gallery sketches](gallery-sketches.png)
*Gallery sketches*

1. **Golf carts.** Automatic driving golf carts with radar and cameras, so no student has to drive. They liked the idea.
2. **Ski lifts.** A cable mechanism at the top and carriers that take one person up the hill. Too expensive, not practical.
3. **Exoskeleton, only legs / thighs.** Hypershell is a good existing brand. I have one of them, but it's in Connecticut. He thinks it's cool, but cost is the problem.

## Experiment & path forward

I decided to try the self-driving path: using existing golf carts to carry students up Gym Hill so we can save them energy. It is the one idea where the resources are already on campus, and it is the one people liked the most.

We will revisit this after Thanksgiving break.

## Reflections

From this experience, I learned how to make good ideas. It is a process: first diverge as much as possible to get a broad view of very different ideas, then gradually narrow it down into practical, interesting, and "good" solutions.

- **Most interesting**: learning not only my story, but also my classmates' stories and their specific problems.
- **Useful**: definitely the methodology above.
- **Surprising**: I did not realize there were so many problems on campus that we can directly work on.

Something I take from this experience is that you can just make it up.
